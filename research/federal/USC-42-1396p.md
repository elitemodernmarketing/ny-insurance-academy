# 42 U.S.C. § 1396p — Liens, adjustments and recoveries, and transfers of assets (Medicaid estate recovery; mandatory since OBRA '93)

- Retrieved/verified: 2026-11-19

Verbatim excerpts used by the content (quotes trimmed with "…"):

- (b)(1) "No adjustment or recovery of any medical assistance correctly paid on behalf of an individual under the State plan may be made, except that the State shall seek adjustment or recovery of any medical assistance correctly paid on behalf of an individual under the State plan in the case of the following individuals:"
- (b)(1)(A) for permanently institutionalized individuals subject to a lien, "the State shall seek adjustment or recovery from the individual's estate or upon sale of the property subject to a lien".
- (b)(1)(B) "In the case of an individual who was 55 years of age or older when the individual received such medical assistance, the State shall seek adjustment or recovery from the individual's estate, but only for medical assistance consisting of— (i) nursing facility services, home and community-based services, and related hospital and prescription drug services, or (ii) at the option of the State, any items or services under the State plan …"
- (b)(1)(C)(ii) Exception for a State "which has a State plan amendment that provides for a qualified State long-term care insurance partnership … which provided for the disregard of any assets or resources— (I) to the extent that payments are made under a long-term care insurance policy …" (also: States with a plan amendment approved as of May 14, 1993).
- (b)(2) "Any adjustment or recovery under paragraph (1) may be made only after the death of the individual's surviving spouse, if any, and only at a time— (A) when he has no surviving child who is under age 21, or … is blind or permanently and totally disabled …"
- (b)(4) "the term \"estate\", with respect to a deceased individual— (A) shall include all real and personal property and other assets included within the individual's estate, as defined for purposes of State probate law; and (B) may include, at the option of the State … any other real and personal property and other assets in which the individual had any legal title or interest at the time of death …"
