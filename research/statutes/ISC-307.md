# NY Insurance Law § 307 — Annual statements; audited financial statements

- Source URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/307
- Revision shown on source: most recent revision from 2014-09-22
- Retrieved/verified: 2026-11-19 (via Firecrawl single-page scrape of NYSenate Open Legislation)

Verbatim excerpts used by the content:

- (a)(1) "Every insurer and every fraternal benefit society that is authorized to do an insurance business in this state … shall file in the office of the superintendent, annually on or before the first day of March, a statement, to be known as its annual statement, executed in duplicate, verified by the oath of at least two of its principal officers, showing its condition at last year-end … Such statement shall be in such form and shall contain such matters as the superintendent shall prescribe."
- (a)(2) The superintendent prescribes the form of the annual statement "as shall seem to him best adapted to elicit a true exhibit of the condition of each such entity, in respect to every matter which he may deem material."
- (a)(4) An insurer "which willfully fails to file an annual statement as required in this section, or willfully fails to reply within thirty days to a written inquiry by the superintendent in connection therewith, shall … be subject, upon due notice and opportunity to be heard, to a penalty of up to two hundred fifty dollars per day of delay, not to exceed twenty-five thousand dollars in the aggregate, for each such failure."
- (b)(1) Every licensed insurer (with a narrow exception for small assessment co-operative property/casualty insurers) "shall be required to file within five months of the end of such calendar year, an annual financial statement … together with an opinion thereon of an independent certified public accountant … Each such insurer shall require the accountant to notify the superintendent if the accountant has determined that the insurer has materially misstated its financial condition as reported to the superintendent or that the insurer does not meet minimum capital or surplus to policyholder requirements."
