# NY Insurance Law § 4220 — Life insurance and annuities; nonforfeiture benefits under defaulted contracts

- Source URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/4220
- Revision shown on source: most recent revision from 2014-09-22
- Retrieved/verified: 2026-11-19 (via Firecrawl scrape of NYSenate Open Legislation)

Note for content authors: § 4220 is a legacy nonforfeiture section. Subsection (a)(1) limits it to "policies and contracts issued prior to the operative date specified in section four thousand two hundred twenty-one". Current individual deferred annuity nonforfeiture rules are in § 4223 (see ISC-4223.md). Content uses § 4220 only for the general concept that a defaulted deferred annuity is converted to a paid-up annuity and that payment of a cash surrender value may be deferred up to six months.

Verbatim excerpts used by the content (quotes trimmed with "…"):

- (a)(1) "This section shall apply only to those policies and contracts issued prior to the operative date specified in section four thousand two hundred twenty-one of this article."
- (b)(1) "Every contract issued after January first, nineteen hundred forty, by any domestic life insurance company which provides for a deferred annuity … except a contract paid for by a single premium, shall provide that if the contract after having been in force for three full years, shall by its terms lapse or become defaulted because any stipulated payment to the company shall not have been made, the reserve on such contract … shall, after deducting a surrender charge … be applied as a net single premium … for the purchase of a paid-up annuity or pure endowment contract".
- (b)(3) A cash surrender value, if provided, "shall be payable to the holder of such contract upon demand therefor and the surrender of such contract within ninety days after the date of lapse or default."
- (c) "The company may provide in any policy or contract that the payment of any cash surrender value may be deferred for not exceeding six months after demand therefor with surrender of the policy or contract".
