# NY Insurance Law § 5104 — Causes of action for personal injury (no-fault tort limitation)

- Source URL: https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/5104
- Revision shown on source: most recent revision from 2026-05-29 (prior revision 2014-09-22)
- Retrieved/verified: 2026-11-19 (via Firecrawl scrape of NYSenate Open Legislation)

Verbatim excerpts used by the content (quotes trimmed with "…"):

- (a) "Notwithstanding any other law, in any action by or on behalf of a covered person against another covered person for personal injuries arising out of negligence in the use or operation of a motor vehicle in this state, there shall be no right of recovery for non-economic loss, except in the case of a serious injury, or for basic economic loss. … No liability for non-economic loss shall be fixed unless and until the trier of fact has determined the existence of a serious injury. In any action to recover non-economic loss pursuant to this article, the trier of fact shall not determine the question of whether an injury is a serious injury until the trier of fact has determined the party or parties at fault."
- (b) "… an insurer which paid or is liable for first party benefits on account of such injuries has a lien against any recovery to the extent of benefits paid or payable by it to the covered person. …"
- (d) [added in the 2026-05-29 revision] "… other than in an action for damages for injuries resulting in death, recovery for non-economic loss shall be limited to one hundred thousand dollars in the case of a serious injury in any action by or on behalf of an injured person who is at fault, … and was (1) operating an uninsured motor vehicle and responsible under article six of the vehicle and traffic law for insuring such motor vehicle, except if a lapse in motor vehicle insurance coverage occurs for a period of time less than thirty days; (2) operating a motor vehicle while impaired at the time of the accident and convicted of such; or (3) operating a motor vehicle in the commission of a felony …"

Note: subsection (d) is new (2026). Because it is recent and its effective date/application was not confirmed, content mentions it only in an "advanced" lesson note and does not build exam answers on it.
